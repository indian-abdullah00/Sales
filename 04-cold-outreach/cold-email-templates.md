## Why it made the cut
These templates follow the patterns that hold up across millions of emails: **short (under ~100 words), simple language, about the buyer, low-friction ask.** Every template below is *illustrative*, written for this library on the source's pattern.

---

## Template 1: Problem-first (Braun style)
> **Subject:** ramping new reps
>
> Hi Alex,
>
> Saw you're hiring 3 SDRs.
>
> When teams double headcount, new reps often take 5+ months to sound like the top performers, because the best talk tracks live in a few people's heads.
>
> We help teams turn their best calls into training, so new reps ramp in weeks.
>
> Open to learning more?
>
> Josh

**Why:** a real trigger (hiring), a common problem, one line about the solution, and an interest-based ask.

---

## Template 2: "Observation → Problem → Credibility → Ask" (Lavender-friendly)
> **Subject:** Q3 board deck
>
> Hi Dana,
>
> Noticed you just added NetSuite alongside Stripe.
>
> Most finance leaders tell us that's when month-end close starts stretching, because revenue has to be reconciled across both by hand.
>
> We helped a similar company cut close from 9 days to 3.
>
> Worth a look?

---

## Template 3: The Referral Email (Aaron Ross, *Predictable Revenue*)
> **Subject:** quick question
>
> Hi Jamie,
>
> I'm trying to find the right person who handles [area] at [Company]. Would you mind pointing me in the right direction?
>
> Thanks,
> Sam

**Why:** it asks for a small favor, not a meeting. The famous early-Salesforce outbound play.

---

## Template 4: The breakup / no-oriented email (Voss style)
> **Subject:** have you given up on this?
>
> Hi Chris,
>
> Haven't heard back, so I'm guessing [problem] has dropped down the list.
>
> Have you given up on fixing it this year?

**Why:** a no-oriented question (Voss) gets replies from people who ignored a yes-oriented one. Saying "no, we haven't given up" feels safe.

---

## Template 5: The "Give, don't ask" email (founder style)
> **Subject:** what 20 support leaders told me
>
> Hi Priya,
>
> I interviewed 20 heads of support about ticket tagging. Three things kept coming up. Summary attached (no pitch).
>
> If any of it matches what you're seeing, happy to compare notes.

---

## Checklist before you send
- [ ] Under 100 words? (Ideally 50–75)
- [ ] Would a 12-year-old understand it?
- [ ] More "you" than "we"?
- [ ] One clear, low-friction ask?
- [ ] Is the personalization *relevant*, not just "I saw you went to State U"?
- [ ] Does it read well on a phone?
