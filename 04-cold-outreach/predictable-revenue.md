# *Predictable Revenue* (the Salesforce outbound model)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Type** | Book |
| **Author / Origin** | Aaron Ross and Marylou Tyler |
| **Year** | 2011 |
| **Field** | SaaS outbound |
| **Evidence tier** | **B: Proven at scale** (Ross's outbound team at Salesforce is credited with generating about $100M in recurring revenue) |
| **Original** | *Predictable Revenue*; [predictablerevenue.com](https://predictablerevenue.com/) |

## Why it made the cut
It's the book that created the modern SaaS sales org structure: **SDRs/BDRs, AEs, and customer success as separate, specialized roles.** Nearly every software company's sales team follows this model.

## Core ideas
1. **Specialize roles:**
   - Inbound lead qualification (market response reps)
   - Outbound prospecting (SDRs: "Cold Calling 2.0")
   - Account executives (close)
   - Customer success (retain and expand)
2. **Cold Calling 2.0:** prospect with **short emails to senior people asking for a referral**, rather than cold-calling into the void.
3. **The "Seeds, Nets, and Spears"** lead types:
   - *Seeds*: word of mouth, customer success
   - *Nets*: marketing / inbound
   - *Spears*: targeted outbound
4. **Never have salespeople prospect.** Closers close; prospectors prospect.

## Key dialogue: the referral email (the book's famous template)
> **Subject:** Appropriate person
>
> Hello Jane,
>
> I'm trying to figure out who is in charge of [area] there, and how I might get in touch with them.
>
> Would you mind pointing me toward the right person, and the best way to reach them?
>
> Thanks,

## Watch-outs
- Exact referral templates have been overused for a decade. Keep the *principle* (a small ask, a senior entry point) and rewrite the copy.
