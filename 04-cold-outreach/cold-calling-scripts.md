## Script 1: The 30MPC-style structure
**Opener → Problem proposition → Discovery → Ask**

> **Opener (permission-based):**
> "Hey Jordan, it's Nick from Acme. This is a cold call. Do you want to hang up, or can I take 30 seconds to tell you why I'm calling, and then you can decide?"
>
> **Problem proposition (name the problems of their role):**
> "I talk with sales leaders at Series B companies, and they usually tell me one of two things: either reps spend hours on manual research before calls, or new reps take five-plus months to ramp. Are either of those on your radar, or not really?"
>
> **If yes, go to discovery:**
> "Tell me more about that. How are you handling it today?" ... "What's the impact?"
>
> **Ask (low-commitment):**
> "Sounds like it's worth a proper conversation. Are you open to 20 minutes next week to see if what we're doing for teams like yours applies? If it doesn't, I'll tell you."

**Why it works:** honesty ("this is a cold call") breaks the pattern the buyer expects. Offering **two problems** lets them pick one rather than reject one. The ask is small.

## Script 2: Josh Braun, "Poke the Bear"
Braun's philosophy: **"Detach from the outcome."** Don't pitch. Ask a question that makes the prospect notice a gap they hadn't seen.

> **Opener with a reason:**
> "Hi Alex, Josh here. I'm calling because I noticed you're hiring three SDRs. Can I ask a quick question about that?"
>
> **Poke the bear (a neutral question that exposes a gap):**
> "When new reps start, how do you currently make sure they're saying the same thing your best reps say?"
>
> *(The prospect often realizes: "...well, we don't, really.")*
>
> **Label and offer, with no pressure:**
> "That's common. Most teams rely on shadowing. Would it be a terrible idea to show you how a few teams are getting new reps to sound like their top performers in weeks rather than months?"

**Signature Braun techniques:**
- **"Is it a terrible idea...?"**: a no-oriented question that's easy to say yes to.
- **Problem-centric, not solution-centric**: "We help with X" → "Teams often struggle with X."
- **Give them permission to say no**, which lowers resistance.

---

## Script 3: Gong-data opener
> "Hi Sam, it's Taylor from Acme. **The reason I'm calling** is [specific reason]..."

Gong's data: **stating the reason for the call early → 2.1x success.** Avoid *"Did I catch you at a bad time?"*, which invites an easy "yes, bye."

## Common objections on the call
| Objection | Response *(illustrative)* |
|---|---|
| "Not interested." | "Totally fair. Most people aren't when I call out of the blue. Out of curiosity, is that because [problem] isn't an issue, or you've already got it handled?" |
| "Send me an email." | "Happy to. So I don't send you something generic, what would make it worth reading? Is [problem A] or [problem B] more relevant?" |
| "We already use [competitor]." | "Makes sense, a lot of teams I talk to do. What do you like about them? ... Is there anything you'd change?" |
| "No budget." | "Understood. If the problem were big enough, is budget something that could be found, or is it truly frozen this year?" |
| "Busy right now." | "Of course. I'll be quick or call back, whichever you prefer. 30 seconds, or Thursday at 2?" |
