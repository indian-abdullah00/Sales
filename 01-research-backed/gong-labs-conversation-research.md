## Why it made the cut
Gong records and transcribes hundreds of millions of B2B sales calls, which makes it the largest public dataset on what top software sellers *actually say* compared with what average sellers say. No other source comes close in sample size.

## Core findings

### Discovery calls
- **11–14 questions** in a discovery call correlated with the best outcomes. Past 15 starts to feel like an interrogation. ([source](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/deal-closing-discovery-call/))
- Top reps **spread their questions across the whole call**. Average reps front-load them as a checklist at the start.
- **Longer buyer answers correlate with closed deals.** Phrase questions so they invite depth.
  - Weak: *"What's your biggest challenge?"*
  - Better: *"Can you help me understand your biggest challenge?"*

### Talk-to-listen ratio
- Original 2016 finding: the "golden ratio" for winning calls was **43% talking / 57% listening**. ([source](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/talk-to-listen-conversion-ratio/))
- 2025 follow-up: the average across all calls is about **60/40 talking**. High performers keep about the same ratio whether they win or lose. Low performers swing from 54% (wins) to 64% (losses). **Talking more is a symptom of a deal going badly.**
- **Cold calls are the exception:** on successful cold calls the rep talks *more* (~55/45), because they have to establish relevance quickly.

### Cold calls ([300M-call dataset](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/the-hidden-power-of-cold-calling-insights-from-300m-calls/))
- **Stating the reason for the call** → **2.1x** higher success rate.
- Successful cold calls are **nearly twice as long** as unsuccessful ones.
- Average reps connect with ~5.4% of prospects; the top quartile connect with ~13.3%.
- The top 5 objections make up **74%** of all cold-call objections ([source](https://www.gong.io/blog/we-found-the-top-objections-across-300m-cold-calls-heres-how-to-handle-them-all)). You can prepare for most of what you'll hear.
- Older Gong cold-call study (~90k calls): the opener *"How have you been?"* beat the baseline, and *"Did I catch you at a bad time?"* did measurably worse. *(Verify the current numbers on Gong before quoting them.)*

### Objections
- Gong's "patience" research: top reps **pause longer after the buyer speaks**, especially after an objection, instead of jumping straight to a rebuttal. ([source](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/handling-sales-objections/))
- Top reps **ask a question back** in response to an objection rather than going straight into a defensive monologue.

## Key dialogue / scripts

**Cold call opener with the reason up front** *(illustrative, built on the Gong findings)*
> "Hi Dana, it's Sam from Acme. I'll be upfront, this is a cold call. The reason I'm calling: I work with VP Finance leaders at mid-market SaaS companies who tell me month-end close is dragging past ten days because revenue data lives in three systems. Is that something you're dealing with, or are you in good shape there?"

**Discovery question that invites depth**
> "Walk me through what happens today from the moment a deal closes to when the revenue hits your books. Where does it break?"

**Pausing after an objection**
> Buyer: "Honestly, we're pretty happy with our current vendor."
> Rep: *(pause, 1–2 seconds)* "That makes sense. What do you like most about them?" *(then:)* "If you could change one thing, what would it be?"

## How to apply it
1. Record your own calls. Check talk ratio, number of questions and longest monologue.
2. Script the first 30 seconds of a cold call so the **reason for calling** comes early.
3. Rewrite discovery questions as open, "help me understand" prompts.
4. Build talk tracks for the **top 5 objections** in your market.

## Watch-outs
- Gong sells the software that measures this, so it has an incentive to publish catchy statistics.
- Most findings are **correlations**. Winning deals may produce long buyer answers rather than long answers producing wins.
- Definitions of "success" vary between posts. Read the original methodology before quoting numbers in decks.
