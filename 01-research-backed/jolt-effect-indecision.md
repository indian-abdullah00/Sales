# The JOLT Effect: Winning Against Customer Indecision

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Type** | Book based on conversation analysis |
| **Author / Origin** | Matthew Dixon and Ted McKenna (Dixon co-authored *The Challenger Sale*) |
| **Year** | 2022 |
| **Field** | B2B sales (heavy software representation) |
| **Evidence tier** | **A: Data-backed** |
| **Original** | *The JOLT Effect* (Portfolio/Penguin); [jolteffect.com](https://www.jolteffect.com/) |

## Why it made the cut
It's based on AI analysis of **2.5 million+ recorded sales conversations**, and it identifies the biggest competitor most deals face: **"no decision."** Most methods are built to beat a rival vendor. This one is built to beat the status quo and the buyer's fear of making a mistake.

## Core findings
- **40–60% of qualified deals are lost to "no decision"**, not to a competitor.
- Two different things cause stalls:
  1. **Status-quo bias**: the buyer doesn't believe change is worth it. *Fix:* make the cost of doing nothing clear (classic sales skill).
  2. **Indecision**: the buyer *wants* to change but fears **making the wrong choice**. *Fix:* JOLT. Classic skills, like piling on more value, often make indecision *worse*.
- The three sources of indecision: **valuation problems** (fear of picking the wrong option), **lack of information** (they feel they need to know more), and **outcome uncertainty** (fear that it won't deliver).

## The JOLT method
| Letter | Behavior | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **J** | **Judge** the level of indecision | Listen for "let me think," "we need to look at more options," "what if...?" |
| **O** | **Offer** your recommendation | "If I were in your shoes, here's what I'd pick and why." |
| **L** | **Limit** the exploration | Narrow the options; stop offering more information. |
| **T** | **Take risk off the table** | Opt-outs, phased rollouts, pilots, guarantees, references. |

## Key dialogue / scripts

**Offer a recommendation** *(illustrative)*
> "You've asked about all three tiers, and honestly most teams your size overthink this. Based on what you told me about the two integrations you need and your team of 40, I'd go with Growth. Most customers like you start there, and you can upgrade any month."

**Limit the exploration**
> "You could look at five more vendors, but from what you've shared, the only two things that matter are the Salesforce sync and SSO. We've shown you both live. Is there anything else that would actually change the decision?"

**Take risk off the table**
> "What if we start with one region for 60 days, with an out clause if adoption doesn't reach 70%? That way you're not betting the whole org on day one."

## How to apply it
1. When a deal stalls, diagnose first: **do they not want to change (status quo), or are they afraid to choose (indecision)?**
2. If it's indecision, **stop adding information.** Give a clear recommendation.
3. Have a ready-made set of risk reducers: pilots, phased contracts, success criteria, references.

## Watch-outs
- If you misread status-quo bias as indecision, you'll push for a decision before the buyer is convinced the problem is worth solving.
