## Why it made the cut
Advice on cold email is mostly opinion. These reports measure reply rates across millions of real emails, so the patterns they agree on are reliable.

## Where the studies agree
| Finding | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Short wins** | Under ~100 words for a first touch is the common guidance. Hunter (34M emails) found **20–39 words** had the highest average reply rate. |
| **Simple language wins** | Lavender-linked analyses report that a **3rd–5th grade reading level** gets meaningfully more replies (secondhand figure: about 67%). |
| **Written for mobile** | Most first opens happen on a phone. Short paragraphs, no walls of text. |
| **About them, not you** | Emails that focus on the buyer ("you" language) beat emails about your company. |
| **Low-friction ask** | An "interest-based" call to action ("Worth a look?") beats a "calendar" one ("Do you have 30 min Tuesday?") on a first touch. |
| **Persona matters** | Lavender: marketing personas reply least (~3.2%); engineering and product reply more (~5.2%). |

## Watch-outs
- Each vendor defines "reply" differently (some count auto-replies or later replies in the thread). Compare *directions*, not exact numbers.
- Deliverability (domain setup, volume) matters more than the copy once you send at scale.
