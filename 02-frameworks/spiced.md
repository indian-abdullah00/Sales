## Why it made the cut
It was designed specifically for **recurring revenue**, where the sale isn't over at signature. The same SPICED notes are handed from the AE to customer success, so expansion and renewal are built on the original reasons for buying. The "Bowtie" model behind it treats the customer lifecycle as one connected funnel.

## The letters
| Letter | Meaning | Example question |
|---|---|---|
| **S** | **Situation** | "What does your team look like today and what tools do you use?" |
| **P** | **Pain** | "What's not working?" |
| **I** | **Impact** (rational and emotional) | "What does that cost you, in money, time, and stress?" |
| **C** | **Critical Event** | "Is there a date by which this has to be solved? What happens then?" |
| **D** | **Decision** (criteria and process) | "How will you decide, and who's involved?" |

## The Critical Event (the most useful idea here)
A critical event is a **date with a consequence**, not a "target date."
- Weak: "We'd like this by Q2."
- Real: "Our SOC 2 audit is March 15. Without automated evidence collection, we fail it and lose the enterprise deal with Acme."

> "What happens on [date] if this *isn't* in place?" separates real urgency from wishful timelines.
