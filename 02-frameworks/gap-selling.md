## Why it made the cut
The simplest modern way to frame a sale: **customers buy to close the gap between their current state and the future state they want.** The size of the gap is the value. No gap, no deal.

## Core ideas
- **Current state**: physical environment (facts) + **problems** + **impact** + root cause + emotions.
- **Future state**: where they want to be, and *why*.
- **The gap**: the distance between the two; the bigger the gap, the more urgent and valuable the deal.
- "**The problem is the only thing that matters.**" If you don't fully understand the problem, you can't sell the solution.
- Relationships come from **expertise**, not likeability: "People buy from experts."
- The **problem identification chart**: for each problem you could solve, list its root cause and its business impact *before* the call.

## Key dialogue *(illustrative)*
> "Before we talk about us at all, I want to make sure I understand where you are today. Walk me through your current onboarding process for new enterprise customers, step by step."
>
> "You mentioned it takes 90 days to go live. What's the impact of that? ... Does that delay revenue recognition? ... By how much, roughly, per customer?"
>
> "And where do you want to be in a year? Why 30 days specifically? What would that unlock?"
>
> "So, if I've got this right: today it's 90 days, costing about $40k in delayed revenue per customer and souring the first impression. You want 30 days so you can hit the expansion targets. That's the gap we'd be closing. Did I miss anything?"

## Watch-outs
- It can turn into a long interrogation. Explain *why* you're asking so many questions.
