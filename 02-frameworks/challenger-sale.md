| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Type** | Book / sales methodology |
| **Author / Origin** | Matthew Dixon and Brent Adamson (CEB/Gartner) |
| **Year** | 2011 |
| **Field** | B2B, enterprise software |
| **Evidence tier** | **B: Proven at scale** (research in [ceb-challenger-research.md](../01-research-backed/ceb-challenger-research.md)) |
| **Original** | *The Challenger Sale*; [Gartner Challenger](https://www.gartner.com/en/sales/insights/challenger-sales-model) |

## Why it made the cut
It shifted B2B sales from "ask what they need" to "**teach them something they didn't know about their own business**." That matters more than ever now that buyers arrive with their research already done.

## The Commercial Teaching Choreography (six steps)
1. **The Warmer**: show you understand their world ("We work with a lot of companies like yours, and we're seeing three challenges...").
2. **The Reframe**: introduce an insight that changes how they see the problem.
3. **Rational Drowning**: data that shows the reframe is real and costly.
4. **Emotional Impact**: make it personal ("Companies like you..." → "You").
5. **A New Way**: describe the better approach (*without* naming your product yet).
6. **Your Solution**: show why you're the best at delivering that new way.

## Key dialogue: illustrative Challenger pitch (revenue intelligence SaaS)
> **Warmer:** "Most CROs we talk to tell us forecast calls feel like theater: reps say 'commit,' and the number still misses."
>
> **Reframe:** "What we've found is that the problem usually isn't rep honesty. It's that forecasts are built on what reps *remember* about deals, not on what buyers actually *did*."
>
> **Rational Drowning:** "Across a few thousand deals we analyzed, opportunities where the economic buyer hadn't joined a single call by stage 3 closed at about a third of the rate of the others, and reps still marked them 'commit.'"
>
> **Emotional Impact:** "So when your board asks why Q3 missed by 12%, the honest answer may be that nobody could see that the deals in commit had never met a decision maker."
>
> **New Way:** "The teams that forecast accurately score deals on buyer engagement signals, not on rep sentiment."
>
> **Solution:** "That's exactly what we built..."

## The other two pillars
- **Tailor**: the CFO hears risk and cost. The VP Sales hears quota attainment. The RevOps lead hears time saved.
- **Take Control**: push back on discounts, ask for access to power, and be willing to say, *"I don't think that would be the right decision for you, and here's why."*

## Watch-outs
- An "insight" that is really a disguised product pitch fails. The reframe has to be true and useful *even if they never buy from you.*
- Being challenging without credibility comes across as arrogance.
