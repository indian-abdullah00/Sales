# Sandler Selling System

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Type** | Sales methodology / training system |
| **Author / Origin** | David Sandler |
| **Year** | Founded 1967; book *You Can't Teach a Kid to Ride a Bike at a Seminar* (1995) |
| **Field** | Cross-industry, widely used in B2B and SMB software |
| **Evidence tier** | **B: Proven at scale** |
| **Original** | [Sandler Training](https://www.sandler.com/) |

## Why it made the cut
It's the most psychologically grounded method for **balancing power between buyer and seller**. It gives you exact language for hard moments: setting the agenda, uncovering pain, budget, and getting a clear yes or no.

## Core ideas
- **The Sandler Submarine**: seven compartments, each sealed before moving on: Bonding & Rapport → **Up-Front Contract** → **Pain** → **Budget** → **Decision** → Fulfillment → Post-sell.
- **Up-Front Contract (UFC)**: agree on purpose, time, both sides' agendas and the possible outcomes *at the start* of every meeting.
- **"No" is fine**; "think it over" is the enemy. Ask for a clear answer either way.
- **Pain funnel**: a sequence of questions that moves from surface pain to its business and personal impact.
- **Negative reverse selling**: move *away* from the sale to lower the buyer's defenses.
- **No mutual mystification**: never leave a meeting unclear about the next step.

## Key dialogue

**The Up-Front Contract**
> "We have 30 minutes. Does that still work? Great. I'd like to ask a few questions about how you handle X today, you'll probably have questions for me, and by the end we can decide together whether it makes sense to keep talking. If it isn't a fit, are you comfortable telling me that? ... And if it is, what would a sensible next step look like?"

**The Pain Funnel** (Sandler's classic sequence)
1. "Tell me more about that..."
2. "Can you be more specific? Give me an example."
3. "How long has that been a problem?"
4. "What have you tried to do about it?"
5. "And did that work?"
6. "How much do you think that has cost you?"
7. "How do you feel about that?"
8. "Have you given up trying to deal with the problem?"

**Negative reverse selling**
> Buyer: "This looks interesting, but I don't know."
> Seller: "Sounds like maybe this isn't a priority right now, and that's fine. Should we just close the file?"
> *(Often the buyer responds by explaining why it IS a priority.)*

## Watch-outs
- Clumsy reverse selling comes across as a mind game. Use it only when it's sincere.
- The term "pain" can come across as negative with senior executives. "Priorities" or "challenges" can work better.
