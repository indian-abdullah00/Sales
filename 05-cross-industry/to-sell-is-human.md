# *To Sell Is Human* (Daniel Pink)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Type** | Book (social science synthesis) |
| **Author / Origin** | Daniel H. Pink |
| **Year** | 2012 |
| **Field** | General persuasion; "non-sales selling" |
| **Evidence tier** | **A/C** (summarizes academic research) |
| **Original** | *To Sell Is Human: The Surprising Truth About Moving Others* |

## Why it made the cut
It brings research from psychology to bear on modern selling, including findings that **challenge sales folklore** (for example, that extroverts make the best salespeople). It's also useful for engineers, founders and anyone who "doesn't do sales."

## Core ideas
- **Everyone is in sales now.** Pink's survey found people spend about 40% of their work time on "non-sales selling" (persuading, influencing).
- **From "buyer beware" to "seller beware":** buyers have as much information as sellers, so honesty and service win.
- **The new ABCs** (replacing "Always Be Closing"):
  - **A**ttunement: take the other person's perspective
  - **B**uoyancy: stay afloat in a sea of rejection (use "interrogative self-talk": *"Can I do this?"* beats *"I can do this!"*)
  - **C**larity: help people see their situation in a new light; *find the right problem* rather than solve the stated one
- **Ambiverts outperform:** research by Adam Grant found that people in the middle of the introvert–extrovert scale outsold strong extroverts.
- **The pitch formats:** the one-word pitch, the question pitch, the rhyming pitch, the subject-line pitch, the Twitter pitch, and the **Pixar pitch**:
  > "Once upon a time ___. Every day, ___. One day ___. Because of that, ___. Because of that, ___. Until finally, ___."

## Key dialogue: the Pixar pitch for a SaaS product *(illustrative)*
> "Once upon a time, support teams tagged every ticket by hand. Every day, agents lost an hour to it. One day, we trained a model on 10 million tickets. Because of that, tags became automatic. Because of that, teams saw trends a week earlier. Until finally, support became the first to know what customers needed."
