## Why it made the cut
Pitching to investors is sales at its most demanding: a skeptical, expert buyer who sees thousands of pitches and says "no" more than 99% of the time. The discipline it forces (**clarity, the "why now," proof of traction**) improves any software sales pitch.

## YC (Michael Seibel): the core rules
1. **Clarity above all.** Say what you do in **one or two plain sentences**. No jargon.
   > "We make X for Y." (e.g., *"We help small restaurants take online orders without paying 30% commissions."*)
2. **Lead with your strongest point** (often traction).
3. **Simple slides, big text.** A deck is a visual aid, not a document.
4. **The order:** what you do → traction → unique insight → business model → market → team → the ask.
5. **Practice the 2-minute version.** If you can't pitch it in 2 minutes, you can't pitch it in 20.

## Sequoia: the classic business-plan outline
1. Company purpose (one line)
2. Problem
3. Solution
4. **Why now** (the change that makes this possible today)
5. Market size
6. Competition
7. Product
8. Business model
9. Team
10. Financials

## The Airbnb seed deck (2008/09): famous for its simplicity
- **Problem:** price is a concern for travelers booking online; hotels leave you cut off from the city and its culture.
- **Solution:** a web platform where users rent out their space to host travelers.
- **Market validation:** cited the size of Couchsurfing and Craigslist temporary-housing listings as evidence of demand.
- **Market size:** TAM/SAM/SOM framing.
- **Business model:** a percentage commission on each booking.
- **Competition and advantages**, plus press and user testimonials.

**Lesson:** each slide has *one* idea. Market validation borrowed proof from existing behavior (people were already doing this on Couchsurfing and Craigslist).

## Paul Graham: "How to Convince Investors"
> Convince yourself first. The most convincing pitch is one where you clearly believe you're building something huge, and can explain why.

He argues the pitch's core is: (1) **what you're building is formidable** and (2) **it could become huge.**

## Lessons that carry over to software sales
| Investor lesson | Sales equivalent |
|---|---|
| One-sentence clarity | Your positioning statement |
| "Why now" | Zuora's "change in the world" / the critical event |
| Traction as proof | Customer stories, references |
| Market validation from existing behavior | "Your team already spends 10 hours a week doing this by hand" |
