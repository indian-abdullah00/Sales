# Keynote Pitch: Steve Jobs' iPhone Launch, Macworld 2007

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Type** | Product launch keynote |
| **Author / Origin** | Steve Jobs, Apple |
| **Year** | January 9, 2007 |
| **Field** | Consumer technology / product launch |
| **Evidence tier** | **B: Proven at scale** (widely regarded as the greatest product pitch of the modern era) |
| **Original** | Macworld San Francisco 2007 keynote (widely available on YouTube); Carmine Gallo, *The Presentation Secrets of Steve Jobs* (2009) |

## Why it made the cut
It's the benchmark for presenting a product to an audience. Every technique in it (**the setup, the reveal, the villain, the rule of three, the demo**) is used by today's best SaaS launch presentations and sales demos.

## The structure
1. **Frame history:** "Every once in a while, a revolutionary product comes along that changes everything." He reminds the audience of Apple's earlier revolutions (the Macintosh in 1984, the iPod in 2001).
2. **The rule of three with a twist:**
   > "Today, we're introducing three revolutionary products of this class. The first one is a widescreen iPod with touch controls. The second is a revolutionary mobile phone. And the third is a breakthrough Internet communications device."
   >
   > "An iPod, a phone, and an Internet communicator. An iPod, a phone... are you getting it? **These are not three separate devices. This is one device, and we are calling it iPhone.**"
3. **The villain:** existing smartphones, "not so smart and not so easy to use," with their **plastic keyboards** fixed in place whether or not you need them.
4. **The solution to the villain:** get rid of the buttons and use a giant screen, with the finger as the pointing device.
5. **The live demo:** scrolling, pinch-to-zoom, calling, maps. **Showing beat telling.**
6. **Social proof and partners:** carrier and executive appearances.

## Techniques you can use in a software pitch
| Technique | Application |
|---|---|
| **A one-line headline** | Every pitch should have a phrase people repeat ("1,000 songs in your pocket") |
| **Rule of three** | Three value themes, three proof points |
| **The setup before the reveal** | Build the problem before showing the product |
| **A clear villain** | The old way of working (spreadsheets, forms, on-prem) |
| **Demo the "aha" live** | One wow moment beats ten features |
| **Simple slides** | One idea, one image, very few words |

## Watch-outs
- A keynote is a one-to-many show. In a sales conversation the buyer should talk more ([Gong](../01-research-backed/gong-labs-conversation-research.md): ~43/57). Use these techniques in the *presentation* part, not in discovery.
