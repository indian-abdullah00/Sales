| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Type** | Book / negotiation method |
| **Author / Origin** | Chris Voss (former lead FBI international kidnapping negotiator) and Tahl Raz |
| **Year** | 2016 |
| **Field** | Hostage negotiation → business and sales |
| **Evidence tier** | **C: Expert practitioner** (tested in life-or-death negotiations; very widely adopted in sales) |
| **Original** | *Never Split the Difference*; [The Black Swan Group blog](https://www.blackswanltd.com/the-edge) |

## Why it made the cut
It's the most useful view from outside sales. Hostage negotiation is the highest-stakes "sale" there is, and its tools (**tactical empathy, labeling, calibrated questions**) carry over directly to discovery, objections and pricing in software deals.

## The toolkit
| Tool | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Tactical empathy** | Show you understand their perspective (not that you agree) | — |
| **Mirroring** | Repeat the last 1–3 words as a question | Buyer: "We need it live before the audit." You: "Before the audit?" |
| **Labeling** | Name the emotion or dynamic | "It seems like there's pressure coming from above on this." |
| **Accusation audit** | List the negatives they might be thinking, before they say them | "You're probably thinking this is just another vendor who'll overpromise..." |
| **Calibrated questions** | Open "how/what" questions that make them solve your problem | "How am I supposed to do that?" · "What's the biggest challenge you face?" |
| **"That's right"** | The breakthrough moment, when they feel truly understood (≠ "you're right," which is a brush-off) | Summarize their situation until they say "that's right." |
| **No-oriented questions** | "No" feels safe; ask questions where "no" moves things forward | "Is now a bad time to talk?" · "Would it be ridiculous to...?" |
| **Late-night FM DJ voice** | A calm, slow, downward tone to lower tension | — |
| **Ackerman bargaining** | Offers at 65% → 85% → 95% → 100% of your target, ending on a precise, non-round number | "$37,850" |
| **Black Swans** | Unknown unknowns that change the deal; uncover them through listening | — |

## Key dialogue in a software deal *(illustrative)*

**Accusation audit before the price conversation:**
> "Before I share pricing, you're probably going to think it's more than you expected, and that we're the expensive option. You might even feel I've wasted your time."

**Calibrated question against a discount demand:**
> Buyer: "We need 30% off or we can't do it."
> Seller: "How am I supposed to do that?" *(pause)*
> Buyer: "...Well, maybe if we signed for two years."

**Labeling a stall:**
> "It seems like something changed since our last call."

**The no-oriented re-engagement email:**
> "Have you given up on fixing [problem]?"

**Getting to "That's right":**
> "So, let me make sure I've got it. You're under pressure to cut costs, you've been burned by a vendor that never delivered, and now you have to justify any new tool to a CFO who's skeptical of software spend."
> Buyer: "**That's right.**"

## Watch-outs
- These are hostage tools. Used mechanically, they come across as manipulative to sophisticated buyers. The empathy part has to be real.
- Win/win isn't Voss's frame, but software sales relies on *long-term* relationships (renewals). Don't win the negotiation and lose the customer.
