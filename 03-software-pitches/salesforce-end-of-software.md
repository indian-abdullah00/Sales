# Salesforce: "The End of Software"

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Type** | Category-creation pitch / marketing campaign |
| **Author / Origin** | Marc Benioff, Salesforce |
| **Year** | 1999–2000s |
| **Field** | Enterprise SaaS |
| **Evidence tier** | **B: Proven at scale** |
| **Original** | Marc Benioff and Carlye Adler, *Behind the Cloud* (2009) |

## Why it made the cut
It's the founding pitch of the SaaS industry. Salesforce didn't sell CRM features against Siebel. It sold **an enemy: "Software"** (meaning on-premise installs, long implementations and shelfware) and positioned itself as the alternative. It's still the textbook case of **selling a category rather than a product.**

## The pitch
- **The enemy:** software itself (expensive licenses, servers, multi-year rollouts, failed implementations).
- **The logo:** the word "SOFTWARE" with a red line through it (the "No Software" logo).
- **The promised land:** business applications delivered over the internet like Amazon, paid for by subscription, live in weeks.
- **The guerrilla stunt:** Salesforce staged a mock protest, with hired actors carrying "No Software" signs, outside a Siebel user conference. It earned press coverage far beyond what its marketing budget could have bought.

## Lessons for software pitches
1. **Pick a fight with an old way of doing things, not with a person.** Buyers can blame the old model rather than themselves.
2. **Make the change visible and simple.** A single symbol (the crossed-out "Software") carried the whole message.
3. **Proof through ease:** free trials and fast setup *were* the argument.

## Key dialogue *(illustrative, in the style of the era)*
> "How long did your last CRM rollout take? Eighteen months? And how many of your reps actually use it today? ... What if you could be live in four weeks, pay per user per month, and stop running servers altogether?"
