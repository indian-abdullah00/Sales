## Why it made the cut
In software, the demo is where most deals are won or lost, yet most reps run a tour of features. Peter Cohan's *Great Demo!* method is the standard reference for how to do it properly.

## Core principles
1. **"Do the Last Thing First"** (Cohan): open with the **end result** the buyer cares about (the finished dashboard, the report, the resolved ticket), *then* show how you got there.
2. **Demo only what was uncovered in discovery.** Each screen should map to a pain the buyer stated.
3. **The "Illustration"**: one striking screen shown early that sums up the value.
4. **Less is more**: stop at "that's what I need." Extra features add confusion and objections.
5. **Check in throughout.** Gong data suggests demos that turn into a two-way conversation do better than long one-way walk-throughs.

## The demo structure *(illustrative)*
> **1. Recap (2 min):** "Last time you told me three things: month-end takes 10 days, the board deck slipped twice, and your analysts spend half their week reconciling. Is that still right? Anything to add?"
>
> **2. The last thing first (1 min):** "Here's what your board revenue report would look like, reconciled automatically as of this morning."
>
> **3. Show how, pain by pain (15 min):** "You mentioned the Stripe and Salesforce mismatch. Here's where that gets resolved..."
> → *Check in:* "How does that compare with what your analysts do today?"
>
> **4. Stop and ask (3 min):** "Of what you've seen, what would matter most to your team?"
>
> **5. Next step (2 min):** "Usually the next step is a 30-minute session with your controller to run your own data through it. Who else should be there?"

## Watch-outs
- **Don't demo before discovery.** "Just show me the product" is a request to manage, not obey: *"Happy to. So I show you the right 10% rather than all of it, can I ask three quick questions?"*
